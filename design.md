# Pulse AI Enterprise Design System

## Authority and implementation contract

This file is the authoritative visual and interaction system for Pulse AI. Future agents and contributors must follow it when implementing or reviewing application UI.

The system is an **Operate** experience: employees complete time entry, managers make accountable decisions, HR maintains workforce data, Finance prepares controlled outputs, and directors scan organizational health. Task clarity, information integrity, permission boundaries, and accessibility outrank decorative expression.

When a requirement is unresolved, represent it as configurable or explicitly pending. Do not invent payroll rules, approval levels, invoice behavior, salary visibility, or AI authority. If an implementation conflicts with this document, bring the implementation into compliance or update this document through an explicit design decision. Do not create undocumented parallel tokens or variants.

## Design thesis: the operations register

Pulse AI should feel like a modern operational register: a calm workspace where every period has an owner, state, evidence, exception history, and next action.

The recognizable language is:

- White work surfaces over a cool-gray canvas.
- Deep slate text with restrained cobalt reserved for actions and selection.
- Tabular numerals for hours, totals, rates, dates, and identifiers.
- Explicit status labels with icons and text, never color alone.
- Thin borders and selective rails instead of decorative shadows.
- Exception-first review: warnings and anomalies live beside affected records.
- Dense tables where comparison matters; spacious forms where completion matters.

The signature element is the **pulse rail**: a 3px vertical rule on the leading edge of a row, card, or alert when state needs emphasis. Its color comes from a semantic token. It is functional, never ornamental, and appears only on the affected object.

The system must not resemble an e-commerce storefront, consumer catalogue, flashy startup, or generic neon AI dashboard.

## Product principles expressed in UI

1. **Action before decoration.** Every page reveals the next meaningful action.
2. **State is evidence.** Status includes a label, icon, timestamp, and responsible party when relevant.
3. **Scope stays visible.** Period, team, project, department, and organization filters remain apparent wherever they affect data.
4. **Exceptions stay local.** Validation and anomaly information appears next to the entry or decision it concerns.
5. **AI remains accountable.** AI content is labeled, grounded in visible data, and distinct from authoritative system state.
6. **Density follows the task.** Review queues may be compact; entry, correction, and confirmation flows must breathe.

## Color system

### Primary and secondary

- `{colors.primary}` is for the primary action, selected navigation, active filters, and selection emphasis.
- `{colors.primary-hover}` and `{colors.primary-active}` are interaction states only.
- `{colors.primary-soft}` supports selected rows, tabs, and informational selection surfaces.
- `{colors.secondary}` is for strong neutral hierarchy and secondary filled actions.
- Do not use large blue gradients, blue page backgrounds, or decorative blue glows.
- One prominent filled primary action per decision region is the default.

### Surfaces and text

- Application canvas: `{colors.canvas}`.
- Sidebar, header, cards, tables, dialogs, and drawers: `{colors.surface}`.
- Alternating rows, quiet panels, and skeletons: `{colors.surface-subtle}`.
- Inverse surfaces are reserved for compact command surfaces, not dashboards.
- Body text uses `{colors.text-primary}` or `{colors.text-secondary}`.
- `{colors.text-tertiary}` is metadata, never critical instruction.
- Placeholder and disabled text must not be used as readable body copy.

### Semantic tokens

| Role | Foreground | Soft surface | Border | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | `{colors.primary}` | `{colors.primary-soft}` | `{colors.primary-border}` | Actions and selection |
| Secondary | `{colors.secondary}` | `{colors.secondary-soft}` | `{colors.border-strong}` | Neutral hierarchy |
| Success | `{colors.success}` | `{colors.success-soft}` | `{colors.success-border}` | Completed operation |
| Warning | `{colors.warning}` | `{colors.warning-soft}` | `{colors.warning-border}` | Correctable risk |
| Error | `{colors.error}` | `{colors.error-soft}` | `{colors.error-border}` | Failure or blocking validation |
| Info | `{colors.info}` | `{colors.info-soft}` | `{colors.info-border}` | Explanatory system message |
| Pending | `{colors.pending}` | `{colors.pending-soft}` | `{colors.pending-border}` | Awaiting action |
| Approved | `{colors.approved}` | `{colors.approved-soft}` | `{colors.approved-border}` | Approved and locked |
| Rejected | `{colors.rejected}` | `{colors.rejected-soft}` | `{colors.rejected-border}` | Rejected workflow state |
| Anomaly | `{colors.anomaly}` | `{colors.anomaly-soft}` | `{colors.anomaly-border}` | Statistical or AI flag |

Approved uses success hues but remains labeled **Approved**. Rejected uses error hues but remains labeled **Rejected**. Warnings represent known rules; anomalies represent statistically unusual patterns. Never merge these concepts.

### Contrast and non-color cues

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and essential UI graphics.
- Every semantic state includes an icon and written label.
- Charts combine color with labels, patterns, shapes, or annotations.
- Focus remains visible on every surface.
- Do not layer tertiary gray text on disabled gray surfaces when contrast fails.

Use Manrope as the preferred family, then Inter and the system sans-serif stack. Use monospace only where alignment improves numeric comparison: hours, totals, rates, dates in dense tables, identifiers, and audit metadata.

| Token | Use |
|---|---|
| `{typography.display-lg}` | Rare executive overview title |
| `{typography.display-md}` | Standard page title |
| `{typography.heading-lg}` | Major page section |
| `{typography.heading-md}` | Card, panel, or dialog heading |
| `{typography.heading-sm}` | Dense component or table group |

| `{typography.body-md}` | Default UI and form text |
| `{typography.body-emphasis}` | Key inline value or instruction |

| `{typography.label-sm}` | Badge and compact metadata |
| `{typography.caption}` | Timestamps and helper text |
| `{typography.data-md}` | Table totals, dates, hours, codes |
| `{typography.data-lg}` | KPI value |
| `{typography.button}` | Sentence-case button labels |

Rules:

- Buttons and navigation use sentence case, never forced uppercase.
- Page titles normally fit on one line and never exceed 40px.
- Use tabular numerals for vertically comparable values.
- Do not reduce operational text below 12px.
- Use more space above a heading than below it.
- Truncate only when the full value remains accessible.

## Spacing, shape, and elevation

- 4-8px: icon gaps and tight metadata.
- 12px: compact control groups.
- 16px: standard component padding.
- 20-24px: cards and panels.
- 32px: major page groups.
- 48px: distinct page sections.
- 64px: unusually spacious overview pages only.

Do not use marketing-scale 80px gaps inside the authenticated app.

- Inputs and buttons: `{rounded.sm}`.
- Cards, tables, drawers, and alerts: `{rounded.md}`.
- Large dialogs and prominent empty states: `{rounded.lg}`.
- Pills are reserved for status, compact filters, and avatars.
- Primary buttons are not pills.

Default surfaces use borders, not shadows. `{elevation.raised}` is for floating menus and sticky summary bars. `{elevation.overlay}` is for dialogs and drawers. Never stack shadow-heavy cards across a dashboard.

## Application shell

### Sidebar

Desktop width is `{layout.sidebar-width}` and collapsed width is `{layout.sidebar-collapsed-width}`.

Structure:

1. Pulse AI product mark/name.
2. Role-relevant primary navigation.
3. Optional secondary group with a labeled divider.
4. Support/settings area at the bottom.

Active items use `{colors.primary-soft}`, `{colors.primary}`, a leading pulse rail, and `aria-current="page"`. Icons are 20px outlined symbols. Collapsed navigation retains accessible names through tooltips and screen-reader text. Destinations do not render when access is absent.

Role navigation:

- Employee: Overview, My timesheet, Notifications, Assistant.
- Manager: Overview, Approvals, Team timesheets, Anomalies, Notifications, Assistant.
- HR: Overview, Employees, Imports, Submission compliance, Notifications, Audit.
- Finance: Overview, Approved hours, Payroll exports, Invoice drafts, Notifications, Audit, Assistant.
- Director: Executive overview, Workforce analytics, Submission health, Project allocation, Assistant.

### Top header

Height is `{layout.header-height}` and it remains sticky on long pages.

Left: compact-layout sidebar trigger, breadcrumbs, and contextual page identity. Right: search, notifications, help, and a user menu showing role and organization scope.

- Use a bottom border, not a persistent shadow.
- Search collapses to an icon below 1024px and opens a command-style layer.
- Show active organization or delegated role when scope can change.
- Never include cart, pricing, promotion, or consumer utility navigation.

### Page frame

- Content is fluid to `{layout.content-max-width}`.
- Padding is 32px desktop, 24px tablet, and 16px mobile.
- Default anatomy: title and scope -> primary action -> summary -> working surface -> supporting detail.
- Use 12 columns on desktop and 4 on mobile.
- Do not center operational pages in narrow marketing columns.

## Dashboard patterns

Every dashboard answers in its first viewport: What period or scope is active? What needs attention? What is the next action?

### Employee dashboard

1. Current-week timesheet state and total hours.
2. Continue timesheet or Review and submit action.
3. Missing-hours or validation summary.
4. Seven-day weekly cadence strip.
5. Recent submissions and notifications.

Do not expose team, salary, or organization-wide data. Do not invent productivity scores.

### Manager dashboard

1. Pending approvals and oldest waiting submission.
2. Missing submissions in assigned scope.
3. Approval queue preview.
4. Validation and anomaly summaries, separated by type.
5. Team hours and project allocation for the period.

The primary action is Review approvals, not Explore.

### HR dashboard

1. Employee master-data health.
2. Submission compliance by department.
3. Import status and row errors.
4. Missing submissions and escalation summary.
5. Recent employee and role changes through audit excerpts.

Salary access is not implied unless explicitly granted.

### Finance dashboard

1. Approved hours ready for export.
2. Exceptions blocking export.
3. Payroll export batches and status.
4. Billable uninvoiced hours and invoice drafts.
5. Traceability from output to source time.

Compensation and billing data must be marked Restricted when shown.

### Director/executive dashboard

1. Organization-wide submission and approval health.
2. Department and project allocation trends.
3. Material exceptions and overdue decisions.
4. Approved-hours and billable-utilization summaries when authorized.
5. Clearly labeled period comparison.

Prefer aggregate data. Individual salary or employee detail is not automatic Director access.

## Controls and navigation patterns

### Buttons

- Primary: `{colors.primary}` background, white text, 40px default or 44px high-emphasis height, `{rounded.sm}`, 16px horizontal padding, verb-first sentence-case label.
- Secondary: white background, `{colors.border-strong}` border, `{colors.text-secondary}` text.
- Destructive: `{colors.error}` only for destructive or irreversible consequences; label the exact action.
- Text actions: `{colors.link}` with underline on hover and visible focus.
- Icon-only buttons: minimum 44px target, accessible name, and tooltip.
- Never hide a primary workflow action behind an icon.

### Inputs and search

- Control height is `{layout.control-height}`.
- Border is `{colors.border-strong}`; focus is a 2px `{colors.focus}` ring with 1px separation.
- Labels remain visible above fields. Placeholders are examples, not labels.
- Helper and error text remain programmatically associated.
- Search includes icon, clear action when populated, keyboard submit, explicit scope, and a no-results state.

### Filters

- Place the filter bar immediately above the affected data.
- Keep period, status, employee/team, department, and project visible when primary.
- Secondary filters may live in a popover or drawer.
- Active filters render as removable labeled chips.
- Show Clear all only when two or more filters are active.
- Preserve frequently reused filters only when Reset to default remains available.

### Pagination

- Desktop: range, total when known, page size, previous/next, and numbered pages.
- Mobile: range plus previous/next.
- Preserve filters and sorting across pages.
- Restore focus to the updated result region.
- Do not use infinite scroll for approvals, audit, payroll, or invoices.

### Status badges

Badge anatomy is icon + written label, with optional metadata outside the pill.

| State | Token | Icon |
|---|---|---|
| Draft | Secondary | Document/edit |
| Submitted | Info | Send |
| Pending review | Pending | Clock |
| Approved | Approved | Check circle |
| Rejected | Rejected | X circle |
| Reopened | Warning | Unlock/rotate |
| Missing | Error | Calendar alert |
| Warning | Warning | Alert triangle |
| Anomaly | Anomaly | Scan/spark plus label |
| Disabled | Disabled | Minus/lock |

Badges use 12px semibold type, 6px vertical and 10px horizontal padding, pill radius, and minimum 24px height. Never use an unlabeled color dot.

## Data and work components

### KPI cards

- 1px border, `{rounded.md}`, 20-24px padding.
- Label, `{typography.data-lg}` value, comparison, then optional compact trend.
- Always define unit and period.
- Use semantic color only when the value carries that meaning.
- Avoid decorative icons on every KPI and walls of tiny equal cards.

### Employee cards

Use for small action-oriented groups, not large workforce lists. Include 36-40px avatar/initials, name, designation, department/team, current timesheet state, and one contextual action. Omit salary, billing rate, sensitive identifiers, and contact detail unless the current task and permission require them.

### Data tables

- Header height 44px; standard row height `{layout.table-row-height}`.
- Sticky headers on long tables.
- Text left, numeric values right, status consistently aligned.
- Tabular numerals for hours, currency, percentages, dates, and IDs.
- Sticky identifying columns when horizontal scroll is unavoidable.
- Hover uses `{colors.surface-subtle}`; selection uses `{colors.surface-selected}`, checkbox, and pulse rail.
- Sorting has visible direction and accessible announcements.
- Bulk actions appear only after selection and state the affected count.
- Critical validation and approval state never lives only in a tooltip.

### Timesheet entry grid

The grid is an editing tool, not a generic spreadsheet.

Desktop anatomy:

- Sticky Project, Task, and optional Billable columns.
- Seven day columns labeled with weekday and date.
- Final total column.
- Weekly total and expected-hours summary in a sticky footer.
- Add row at the end of the editable group.
- Inline per-cell validation without unstable layout shifts.

Interaction:

- Arrow and Tab navigation between hour cells.
- Accept only the confirmed hour format; do not reinterpret ambiguous text.
- Show Saving, Saved, or Save failed near the grid title.
- Briefly highlight modified cells without decorative animation.
- Blocking rules use error; non-blocking policy conditions use warning; statistical findings use anomaly only after evaluation.
- Update totals immediately through deterministic logic.
- Approved periods are read-only with lock and approval metadata.

Mobile converts the grid into day-grouped entry cards or a focused day editor while preserving project context and weekly totals. Never shrink seven editable columns below usable touch size.

### Timesheet submission

Submission is a review step:

1. Show period, total, expected hours, blocking errors, warnings, and acknowledged exceptions.
2. Disable Submit only for blocking conditions and explain why.
3. Require warning acknowledgement only when business rules require it.
4. Confirm what becomes read-only and who reviews it.
5. On success, show submitted time, approver, and next step.

Use the label Submit timesheet, not vague Continue or Confirm labels.

### Manager approval queue

- Group by period and expose scope.
- Default columns: Employee, Team, Total, Expected, Validation, Anomalies, Submitted, Age, Status, Action.
- Filters: period, team, status, anomaly severity, overdue age.
- Review view: daily totals, project allocation, validations, anomalies, previous decisions, employee notes.
- Keep Approve and Reject separate and labeled.
- Rejection requires a reason.
- Bulk approval is limited to eligible records and confirms count and scope.
- An anomaly never automatically rejects or approves.

### Validation and alert patterns

#### Blocking validation error

Error-soft surface, error border, icon, concise title, exact repair instruction, and a control that focuses the affected entry.

#### Missing-hours alert

Use warning before a deadline and error for an overdue required submission when policy defines it as missing. State the missing amount or day, deadline, and action.

#### Duplicate-hours alert

Use warning or error according to configured policy. Show or link both records. Never call a deterministic duplicate an AI finding.

#### Unusual-hours/anomaly alert

Use anomaly tokens and an explicit AI/statistical label. Show reason, meaningful severity, comparison baseline, affected records, and feedback actions: Valid, Incorrect, Needs clarification. Include: This flag does not change approval automatically.

### Notifications

- Header drawer on desktop; full page on mobile.
- Group by Today, Earlier this week, and Older.
- Each item has category icon, title, action-oriented message, timestamp, read state, and destination.
- High priority uses a pulse rail; ordinary unread uses a subtle blue surface.
- Settings separate reminders, approvals, finance outputs, and system/security events.

### Import preview

Use four stages: Upload -> Map -> Validate -> Commit.

- Show file name, type, size, and row count.
- Map source columns to required Pulse AI fields.
- Preview valid, warning, invalid, duplicate, and unchanged rows.
- Row errors show field, supplied value, reason, and correction.
- Commit remains unavailable while blocking errors exist.
- Explain whether the operation is transactional.
- Final result reports created, updated, skipped, and failed counts with downloadable errors.

### Analytics charts

- State measure, unit, period, scope, and comparison basis.
- Use blue for the principal series, slate for comparison, and semantic colors only for semantic data.
- Reserve anomaly purple for anomaly measures.
- Limit categorical palettes to six series.
- Avoid 3D charts, radial gauges, excessive donuts, gradient fills, and unexplained dual axes.
- Provide an accessible summary or table.
- Tooltips supplement rather than contain critical values.

Preferred forms: line for trends, horizontal bar for group comparisons, stacked bar for workflow composition, heatmap for allocation or missing concentration, and bullet/progress bar for confirmed target versus actual.

## Dialogs and system states

### Modal dialogs

- Use for bounded decisions or small forms, not complete workflows.
- Widths: 480px small, 640px standard, 800px only for comparison-heavy content.
- Structure: title, consequence/context, body, actions.
- Primary action is rightmost on desktop and full-width last on mobile.
- Move focus in, trap it, then return it to the invoker.
- Escape does not discard unsaved or destructive work silently.

### Confirmation dialogs

- Name the object and action: Approve 12 timesheets, Reject Priya's timesheet, Reopen week 34.
- Explain downstream effects such as locking, export eligibility, or resubmission.
- Use destructive styling only for destructive consequences.
- Do not use Are you sure? as the complete message.

### Empty states

Distinguish no data, no results, no permission, and no required action. Include a small line icon, specific heading, one-sentence explanation, and one relevant action when available.

Examples:

- No pending approvals: Your assigned team has no timesheets awaiting review.
- No search results: No employees match the current search and filters.
- No anomalies: No unusual entries were flagged for this period.

Do not celebrate missing data when it may indicate a permission or configuration issue.

### Loading and skeleton states

- Skeleton geometry matches final content.
- Use neutral gray with a restrained pulse of at least 1.2 seconds.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` with a static skeleton.
- Keep existing data visible during safe refetches.
- Show measurable progress for imports and exports.
- Do not show a full-page spinner for a local table update.

### Error states

Every error explains what failed, what was preserved, and what happens next.

- Field errors stay beside fields.
- Section errors remain within the affected panel.
- Page errors provide Retry and safe navigation.
- Permission errors avoid leaking protected record existence.
- Save failures preserve input and state whether retry will occur.
- Technical references may be copyable support IDs, not primary messages.

## AI assistant/chat panel

The assistant is a constrained enterprise tool, not the center of every dashboard.

- Desktop: right drawer, 400-480px wide.
- Mobile: full-screen route.
- Keep the underlying page as visible context on desktop.

Anatomy:

- Header: Pulse AI Assistant, current role/scope, close.
- Context bar: period, team/project scope, and freshness.
- User and assistant messages.
- Role-permitted suggested queries.
- Composer, send action, and concise privacy notice.

Rules:

- Neutral message surfaces; no gradients, glow, robot imagery, or sparkles.
- AI content carries an AI label and links to relevant records or reports where possible.
- Tool results expose filter, period, and scope.
- Never display system prompts, raw SQL, tokens, or authorization internals.
- Read-only behavior is explicit. Future actions require normal UI review and human confirmation outside chat.
- Refusals explain access boundaries without confirming protected records exist.
- Loading says what is being checked, not theatrical thinking language.

## Audit/history timeline

- Use a vertical timeline for one resource and a table for organization-wide audit search.
- Each event includes action, actor, role, timestamp, reason, and changed-fields summary.
- State events use semantic icons with neutral body text.
- Revisions and approval actions may expand in place.
- Redact before/after values outside the viewer's permission.
- Label AI tool activity separately from human decisions.
- Audit presentation is immutable; no edit or delete controls.

## Interaction states

Every interactive component defines Default, Hover, Focus-visible, Active, Selected, Disabled, Loading, and Error when relevant.

Focus-visible uses a 2px `{colors.focus}` ring with at least 1px separation. Never remove focus because hover exists. Disabled controls remain legible and expose a reason when it is not obvious. Prefer actionable validation over disabling when users can correct the condition.

## Responsive behavior

| Name | Width | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile | `< 768px` | Single column, sidebar drawer, full-screen assistant |
| Tablet | `768-1023px` | Sidebar collapsed or drawer, two-column summaries, reduced table columns |
| Desktop | `1024-1439px` | Persistent sidebar, full header, 12-column grid |
| Large desktop | `>= 1440px` | Wider tables, content capped at 1600px |

- Preserve task order before desktop composition.
- Collapse the sidebar below 1024px.
- Hide low-priority columns only when the complete record remains accessible.
- Allow table scroll when comparison requires it and keep identity columns sticky.
- Convert editing grids to focused mobile forms.
- Dialogs and drawers become full-screen sheets on small mobile devices.
- KPI grids collapse four -> two -> one.
- Touch targets are at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels.
- Support 200% zoom without loss of content or operation.

## Accessibility

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA.
- Use semantic landmarks, headings, lists, tables, buttons, and forms.
- Provide a skip link.
- Every function is keyboard operable.
- Announce saves, validation changes, result counts, and asynchronous completion appropriately.
- Tables have accessible names and correct header associations.
- Long-form errors are summarized and linked to fields.
- Charts include text summaries or equivalent tables.
- Icon-only controls have accessible names and visible tooltips.
- Avatars use empty alt text when identity is already adjacent.
- Respect reduced motion, increased contrast, and forced colors where practical.
- Never communicate state through color, position, or motion alone.

## Content and UX writing

- Use direct operational language and verb-first actions.
- Use product terms consistently: Employee, Manager, HR, Finance, Director, timesheet, entry, period, approval, anomaly, payroll export, invoice draft.
- Distinguish error, warning, and anomaly.
- State scope in confirmations and results.
- Use unambiguous localized dates and timezone where deadlines or audit events require it.
- Never imply AI conclusions are authoritative. Use Flagged as unusual, not AI detected fraud.
- Avoid marketing language, exclamation marks, and anthropomorphic assistant copy.

## Iconography and imagery

- Use one outlined icon family such as Lucide at 18-20px.
- Icons support labels and do not replace critical labels.
- Use avatars or initials only where identity improves recognition.
- Avoid stock photography inside the authenticated product.
- Empty-state imagery is a simple line diagram using slate and one semantic accent.
- Do not introduce robots, brains, stars, glowing orbs, circuits, or generic AI imagery.
- HP marks, chevrons, product photography, cart icons, price imagery, sale treatments, and customer-story imagery are prohibited.

## Data density and hierarchy

- Summary before detail, exception before normal state, decision before secondary analysis.
- Use progressive disclosure for advanced filters, history, and model explanation.
- Prefer tables over cards when comparison matters.
- Do not make dashboards entirely from KPI cards.
- Use headings, rules, and whitespace instead of wrapping every group in a card.
- Keep totals close to source records.
- Keep selection, filters, and scope visible during bulk operations.

## Motion

- Hover/focus transitions: `{motion.fast}`.
- Drawers, dialogs, disclosure: `{motion.standard}` to `{motion.deliberate}`.
- Use opacity and translation no greater than 8px.
- Avoid bounce, overshoot, parallax, ambient loops, and animated gradients.
- Preserve content visibility without motion.
- Totals or statuses may use a brief background transition, never counting spectacle.

## Do and do not

### Do

- Use documented tokens rather than ad hoc values.
- Keep primary blue scarce and purposeful.
- Pair semantic labels and icons with color.
- Show period and organizational scope on operational pages.
- Put validation beside affected entries.
- Use tabular numerals for comparable values.
- Design keyboard and mobile behavior with every desktop component.
- Keep AI accountable and subordinate to the workflow.
- Label synthetic demonstration data.

### Do not

- Do not restore HP colors, chevrons, fonts, or language.
- Do not add product cards, pricing tiers, carts, sale badges, testimonials, or marketing footers to the app shell.
- Do not use proprietary fonts.
- Do not use neon gradients, glassmorphism, glowing AI borders, or decorative dark dashboards.
- Do not make every surface a card.
- Do not use color-only status dots.
- Do not hide authorization or scope decisions in the frontend.
- Do not show salary as a default dashboard metric.
- Do not let anomaly styling imply guilt, fraud, or automatic rejection.
- Do not make LLM output visually identical to authoritative records.

## Implementation checklist for future agents

Before implementing a surface, verify:

1. User role, task, data scope, and primary action are known.
2. The surface uses tokens from this file.
3. Default, loading, empty, error, permission, and success states are designed.
4. Keyboard order, focus, labels, announcements, and contrast are defined.
5. Mobile behavior is an adaptation, not a compressed desktop view.
6. Tables, charts, totals, and statuses have accessible text equivalents.
7. Sensitive fields are omitted unless role and task require them.
8. AI content is labeled, scoped, grounded, and non-authoritative.
9. No unresolved business rule was silently invented.
10. The result is checked at desktop and mobile widths against this document.

- Near-black is described as slate, not navy.

- All headings are sans-serif; the erroneous serif reference is removed.
